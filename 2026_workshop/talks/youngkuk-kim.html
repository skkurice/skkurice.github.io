Quantum Materials Research in the Department of Physics at SKKU

Youngkuk Kim

Department of Physics, Sungkyunkwan University

Schedule to be announced

Abstract

Youngkuk Kim

  • Department of Physics, Sungkyunkwan University, Suwon 16419, Republic of Korea

The Department of Physics at Sungkyunkwan University brings together complementary approaches to quantum materials, spanning epitaxial growth, spectroscopy, and theoretical design. This talk will introduce four participating research groups and highlight their research activities.

Woo Seok Choi’s Epitaxial Complex Oxide Lab investigates the growth and physical properties of transition-metal oxide heterostructures. Combining pulsed laser epitaxy with optical spectroscopy, the group explores electronic and magnetic behavior in engineered oxide structures, connecting controlled synthesis with the characterization of emergent phenomena and functional properties.

Youngkuk Kim combines first-principles calculations with effective theories to investigate quantum materials. His research examines how symmetry, topology, quantum geometry, and electronic correlations govern novel phases, with interests including flat bands in kagome and heavy fermion systems, low-dimensional materials, and the control of electronic states by light and electric fields.

Hoon Kim investigates correlated materials through optical and X-ray spectroscopy, including Raman scattering and resonant inelastic X-ray scattering. His group uses pump–probe methods to study dynamical responses and their control, with particular interests in iridates, ruthenates, and routes to engineered quantum states through periodic driving and cavity environments.

Byungmin Sohn combines in situ oxide molecular beam epitaxy with angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy to investigate the electronic structure of low-dimensional correlated oxides. His research connects precisely controlled growth with momentum-resolved measurements, addressing how orbital selectivity, electronic correlations, and reduced dimensionality give rise to Mott transitions and flat bands.

Together, these research directions offer opportunities for SKKU⁠–⁠Rice collaboration linking materials synthesis, experimental characterization, and theoretical understanding to explore and control quantum states in solids.
