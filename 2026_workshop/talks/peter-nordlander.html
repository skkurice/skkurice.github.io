Carrier Multiplication in Plasmonic Photocatalysis

Peter Nordlander

Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rice University

Session 1 · Day 1 · Dec 3 · 10:00 am – 10:30 am

Abstract

Nanoparticle plasmons can serve as efficient generators of hot electrons and holes that can be exploited in a variety of light harvesting applications.[1] The physical mechanism for plasmon-induced hot carrier generation is non-radiative plasmon decay which is a quantum mechanical process in which one plasmon quantum is transferred to the conduction electrons of the nanostructure by creating hot electron-hole pairs. These hot carriers subsequently relax through electron-electron scattering which results in strong carrier multiplication. The lower energies of these carriers can induce vibrational pumping of molecules adsorbed on the catalyst and result in bond breaking. In my talk, I will discuss plasmon-induced hot carrier generation and the time-dependent relaxation of plasmon-induced hot carriers. I will highlight a few important reactions where such carrier multiplication play an important role including Methane Dry and Steam Reforming,[2,3] Ammonia Decomposition,[4] Hydrogenation and Dehydrogenation of Liquid Organic Hydrogen Carriers (LOHC),[5] and CO2 splitting.[6]
