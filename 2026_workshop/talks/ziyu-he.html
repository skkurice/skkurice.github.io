Toward Digital–Analog Quantum Processing with Tunnel-Coupled Erbium Tweezer Arrays

Ziyu He

Graduate Student, Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rice University
Advisor: Prof. Gyu-Boong Jo

Lunch and Poster Session · Day 2 · Dec 4 · 12:15 pm – 2:30 pm

Abstract

Ziyu He1, Chengdong He1, Peng Ren1, Soham Mukherjee1, Yuchao Liu1, Mulin Zheng2, and Gyu-Boong Jo*1,2

  • 1Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rice University, Houston, TX, USA
  • 2Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Rice University, Houston, TX, USA

Optical tweezer arrays provide a versatile platform for programmable quantum simulation, offering flexible lattice geometries and precise control over individual atoms. We are developing a digital–analog quantum processing architecture based on tunnel-coupled optical tweezer arrays, with strongly dipolar erbium atoms as the target system. The proposed architecture combines analog many-body dynamics in a tweezer-defined lattice with digital operations, including tunneling-based mode exchange and entangling gates mediated by Rydberg blockade or dipolar interactions. A functional-zone architecture is envisioned to integrate coherent state preparation, gate operations, and many-body evolution within a single platform.

As an experimental step toward this goal, we present programmable quantum-walk dynamics in a strontium tweezer array. Hybrid SLM–AOD tweezer potentials enable compact lattice geometries and dynamic control of inter-site tunneling through trap modulation. We also outline our second-generation erbium apparatus, designed to improve motional-state control and enhance tunneling rates while extending the platform toward interacting many-body dynamics. Together, these efforts establish a path toward combining discrete quantum operations with continuous analog evolution in a reconfigurable neutral-atom processor.
