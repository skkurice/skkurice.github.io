Advanced Thermal Management Technology Using Nanocomposites

Seunghyun Baik

School of Mechanical Engineering, Sungkyunkwan University

Schedule to be announced

Abstract

Seunghyun Baik*

  • School of Mechanical Engineering, Sungkyunkwan University, Suwon, 16419, Korea

Thermally conductive polymer-matrix nanocomposites have received considerable attention for the advanced thermal management of electronic devices. The thermal interface material (TIM) fills the inevitable gap between the heat source and heat exchanger to decrease contact thermal resistance (Rc). Our first TIM research employed silver (Ag) flakes and multi-walled carbon nanotubes embellished with Ag nanoparticles (nAgMWNTs) in rigid epoxy matrix [1]. The 1-dimensional long nanotubes constructed thermal transport bridges between Ag flake islands, and the contact interface was improved by the coalescence between Ag flakes and Ag nanoparticles pre-functionalized on the surface of nanotubes. This approach provided a very high thermal conductivity (κ) of ~160 W m-1 K-1 as an adhesive type TIM [1]. However, the total thermal resistance (Rt) was large as a pad-type TIM due to the rigid epoxy matrix. We realized that there was a trade-off behavior between κ and Rc with regard to hardness [2]. The research efforts to overcome this trade-off behavior will be discussed including sandwich-structure [2] and healable phase-change TIMs [3]. The TIMs with non-oxidized copper nanoparticles and nanosatellite particles will also be introduced [4, 5]. The tunneling transport between nanosatellite particles dramatically enhances both electrical and thermal transport properties [6-9]. The recent progress in our laboratory will also be introduced.
