Posters / Poster P18

Quantum Simulation of 2D Supersolid with Strongly Dipolar Neutral Atoms

Mulin Zheng

Graduate Student, Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Rice University
Advisor: Prof. Gyu-Boong Jo

Lunch and Poster Session · Day 2 · Dec 4 · 12:15 pm – 2:30 pm

Abstract

Mulin Zheng1, Haoting Zhen2,3, Yifei He3, Ziyu He2, Chuwei Liao2, Chengdong He2, and Gyu-Boong Jo*1,2

  • 1Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Rice University, Houston, TX, USA
  • 2Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rice University, Houston, TX, USA
  • 3Department of Physics, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Hong Kong SAR

Quantum simulation offers a powerful route to studying complex quantum systems that are intractable by classical computation. Ultracold atomic gases are particularly well suited to this task, as their interactions, dimensionality, geometry, and dynamics can all be precisely controlled. Here we show how neutral atoms provide a unique opportunity to address a long-standing question, whether supersolids can exist in two dimensions. A supersolid is a paradoxical phase of matter in which frictionless superfluid flow coexists with crystalline order. Although long sought after, its existence in 2D has remained in question because of the enhanced fluctuations intrinsic to reduced dimensionality. The question is also conceptually connected to high-Tc superconductivity, where the superconducting phase is accompanied by crystalline (charge-density) modulation.

In this poster, we present recent results obtained with a strongly dipolar two-dimensional quantum simulator. Using tunable dipolar interactions with erbium atoms, our platform realizes the long-predicted two-dimensional supersolid phase via roton-driven stripe formation. We probe spatial density modulation with global matter-wave coherence as well as collective-mode measurements, which establishes the new phase of matter.
