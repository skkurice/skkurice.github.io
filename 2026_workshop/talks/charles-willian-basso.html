Posters / Poster P17

Cryogenic apparatus for quantum simulation with 2D ion crystals

Charles Willian Basso

Rice University
Advisor: Prof. Guido Pagano

Lunch and Poster Session · Day 2 · Dec 4 · 12:15 pm – 2:30 pm

Abstract

Charles Willian Basso, Shuzhuo Ale, Evan Miller, Visal So, Roman Zhuravel, and Guido Pagano

In this work, we present the design of a cryogenic trapped-ion apparatus for performing quantum simulations of spin and spin-boson models using two-dimensional 171Yb+ ion crystals. We report the design of a new monolithic ion trap with a geometric configuration that allows the confinement of hundreds of ions. To achieve MHz secular trap frequency and negligible micromotion away from the RF null, the design requires operation with high voltages (500 V) and high RF frequencies (~40 MHz), leading to an increase in the RF power dissipated on the trap. Consequently, we have engineered an ion trap with low capacitance to reduce heat dissipation. To optimize the capacitance, we simulated the surface charge density of the ion trap to identify and mitigate the most significant hot spots of charge accumulation, which contribute most to the system's total capacitance.

We also report finite-element simulations using the software COMSOL to determine the trap frequencies in a stable confinement potential configuration. We developed a numerical method to optimize the range of accessible trap frequency as a function of applied voltages for different geometrical parameters of the ion trap, including trench sizes and shapes, blade angle variations, and electrode dimensions.

We also report on the design of a sapphire PCB board for both the electric routing of DC and RF signals and thermal heat sinking of the dissipated power and on the design of a compact RF helical resonator inside the copper trap holder that fits inside the region whose temperature is 4 K. Finally, we also report the design of an in-vacuum lens for photon collection and individual ion addressing. We anticipate that this design will enable the simulation of two-dimensional spin and spin-boson models with hundreds of ions with individual-ion control.
