Tom Killian

Dean, Wiess School of Natural Sciences; Professor, Physics and Astronomy
Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rice University

Short bio

Thomas C. Killian received an A.B. from Harvard College in 1991, an M.Phil. in physical chemistry from Cambridge University in 1993 as a Marshall Scholar, and a Ph.D. in atomic physics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1999. He was a National Research Council Postdoctoral Fellow at the National Institute of Standards and Technology and joined the Rice University faculty in 2001. At Rice he has served as chair of the Department of Physics and Astronomy, associate dean of strategic planning for the School of Natural Sciences, and deputy speaker of the Faculty Senate. He is dean of the Wiess School of Natural Sciences and professor of physics and astronomy. His group studies ultracold neutral plasmas and ultracold atomic gases using laser-cooled strontium. He is a Fellow of the American Physical Society and has received a David and Lucile Packard Foundation Fellowship and an Alfred P. Sloan Research Fellowship.

Research areas

  • Ultracold neutral plasmas
  • Quantum degenerate atomic gases
  • Ultracold Rydberg atoms and molecules
  • Rydberg-atom synthetic dimensions
